Research

Engineering Microbial Systems for One Health

At the Habgood Lab we are using Engineering Biology tools for microbiome editing.

Our research looks at ways to best deploy editing tools for the control of important One Health pathogens and antimicrobial resistance. Specific research interests include looking into the fate of “edited” microbes in biological systems and how we can develop better models to understand horizontal gene transfer in microbiomes.

01

Engineering Biology

We develop and apply biological engineering tools to manipulate microbial systems. A central question is how editing technologies can be moved beyond isolated laboratory strains and deployed in more complex biological environments.

02

Microbiome Engineering

Microbial communities are dynamic, diverse and highly interconnected. We are interested in how targeted editing can be used to understand and manipulate these communities while accounting for what happens to engineered organisms after intervention.

03

Antimicrobial Resistance

We investigate engineering approaches relevant to antimicrobial resistance, including how genetic information moves through microbial communities and how biological systems might be controlled to reduce the impact of important resistant pathogens.

04

One Health

Our research considers microbial systems in the context of human, animal and environmental health. The aim is to develop approaches that can be understood and evaluated across the biological systems in which they will ultimately operate.

05

Biological Control

We are interested in using biological systems as controllable tools, including approaches for targeting or modifying microbial populations and communities in ways that could have practical applications.
